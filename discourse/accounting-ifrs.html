Accounting & IFRS

A practical Nigerian perspective on financial reporting, accounting judgement and the information that enables investors, boards and management to make sound decisions.

High-quality financial reporting is not simply a compliance exercise. In Nigeria, financial statements sit at the intersection of IFRS Accounting Standards, Financial Reporting Council expectations, tax considerations, corporate governance, audit and the realities of operating in a changing economy. This discourse examines how accounting information can become more useful, comparable and decision-relevant.

Why IFRS matters in Nigeria

The purpose of financial reporting is to communicate economic reality faithfully enough for users to make informed decisions. For Nigerian entities, that requires careful attention to recognition, measurement, presentation, disclosure and professional judgement. It also requires an understanding of the business behind the numbers: contracts, customers, financing arrangements, assets, liabilities, foreign-currency exposures and the assumptions used by management.

Areas of practical attention

The discourse covers revenue recognition, leases, financial instruments, impairment, provisions and contingencies, consolidation, deferred tax, going concern, cash-flow reporting, management-defined performance measures, accounting estimates and the relationship between financial reporting and internal control. Particular attention is given to areas where judgement can materially affect how performance and financial position are understood.

Nigeria-specific reporting considerations

The Nigerian environment can create reporting complexity through foreign-exchange movements, inflationary pressure, changing financing costs, regulatory requirements and sector-specific business models. Entities also need to consider the interaction between statutory reporting, tax records, management accounts and regulatory returns. Strong reporting processes reconcile these different information needs without allowing one purpose to distort another.

What is changing in the reporting landscape

The Financial Reporting Council of Nigeria continues to develop the reporting environment. Its 2026 sustainability reporting roadmap and guideline provide an important direction for entities preparing for IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. IFRS 18 is also scheduled to become effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, bringing significant changes to presentation and disclosure. These developments make reporting architecture, data quality and management readiness increasingly important.

From compliance to decision usefulness

A strong finance function should help management understand what the numbers mean, not merely produce them. That means connecting the general ledger to operational drivers, improving close processes, documenting judgements, strengthening reconciliations and ensuring that board reporting tells a coherent story. The goal is financial information that can withstand scrutiny and support better decisions.
