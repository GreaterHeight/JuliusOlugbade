Research & Publications

A research-oriented space connecting documented scholarship with questions of accounting, taxation, financial inclusion, governance and organisational development in Nigeria.

Research should do more than occupy a bibliography. It should sharpen questions, test assumptions and provide evidence that can improve professional practice. This section presents documented publications and frames them within the wider Nigerian questions they address.

Research themes

The documented work represented here includes personal income taxation and infrastructure development in Lagos State, financial inclusion and stakeholder value in Nigerian deposit money banks, human-capital diversity and faithful representation in accounting information, and the relationship between public revenue and social infrastructure. Together, these subjects sit at the intersection of finance, public policy, accounting and development.

Why Nigerian evidence matters

Imported frameworks can be useful, but Nigerian institutions have their own regulatory structures, market behaviour, infrastructure constraints and social realities. Research grounded in local evidence can reveal where international assumptions hold, where they require adaptation and where new questions need to be asked.

From research to professional practice

The value of research is realised when evidence changes a decision, improves a control, challenges a management assumption or clarifies a policy choice. For practitioners, this means translating findings into questions that boards, finance teams, regulators, educators and business leaders can use.

Research integrity

This page distinguishes documented publications from future editorial ideas. New articles, papers or claims should not be attributed to Dr. Julius Olugbade unless they are supported by a verifiable source. Where research is discussed, the preferred approach is to identify the question, evidence base, method, limitations and practical implication.
